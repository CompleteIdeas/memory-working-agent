MWA · how you set it up and pair your phone · 26 October 2026

From "install" to "it's on my phone and only I can reach it"

MWA lives in one place: your computer, or your Railway account. Your phone and your computer reach it through a private network that only your own devices can join. MWA is never just a website on the open internet. On top of that, each phone gets its own key, and you can take that key away at any time.

The short version

  1. Pick a home for MWA. Your computer is free and keeps your data at home, but it only answers while the computer is on. Railway is always on, for a few dollars a month.
  2. Put it in the box. Install Tailscale on that home and on your phone, and sign both into the same account. Now only your devices can even see MWA. There's no public web address to attack.
  3. Pair your phone. MWA on your computer shows a QR code. Scan it with the phone, tap Pair, tap Install. MWA is on your home screen.
  4. Connect your calendar and email from the phone or the computer, or skip that for now.

After that you just open the MWA icon, anywhere you have signal. That works whenever your computer is awake, or any time if MWA lives on Railway.

Where MWA lives

On your computer

MWA runs quietly in the background on your Windows PC.

  • Cost: free (you pay only the AI usage).
  • Your data stays on your own disk.
  • Reachable only while the computer is on and awake. When it isn't, the phone app opens with what it last saw ("updated 7:30 AM · offline"). Anything you do waits and is sent when the computer is back.
  • Background jobs (morning check-in, the weekday news watch) only run while the computer is on.
recommended for "use it anywhere"

On Railway

MWA runs in your Railway account. Your production copy already does.

  • Cost: a few dollars a month for hosting, plus AI usage.
  • Always on. Check-ins and watches run at night too, and your phone always reaches it.
  • Your data sits on Railway's private storage volume, not on a public server.
  • Made private: it joins your Tailscale network, and its public web address is switched off. So it is reachable only from your devices, exactly like the computer option.

You can start on the computer and move to Railway later. Moving means copying the data folder: memory, settings, sign-ins and the app database.

Why not just a password on a public website? That's how the current Railway copy works. It's the common approach, but the login page is visible to the whole internet, so anyone can try passwords or look for a weakness. With layer 1 there is no login page to find: a stranger's connection never reaches MWA at all. The password and phone keys stay as the second lock, in case a device inside your network is compromised.

Onboarding, step by step

What you'd see, in order. The phone frames show the screens; each step says where it happens. Steps in grey italics are one-time technical chores that a setup helper will do for you where it can.

Part A: set up the home (once, about 10 minutes)

  1. Install MWA. Computer: run the MWA installer; it starts quietly at login. Railway: deploy the MWA template into your account; it creates the service and a private storage volume.
  2. Open the setup page once. Computer: it opens by itself at http://localhost, which only that computer can reach. Railway: a temporary setup link from the Railway dashboard, valid until step 5.
  3. Create your password (or a passkey, so your fingerprint or face signs you in), and add your AI key. The key is saved in MWA's vault; the AI never sees it.
  4. Put it in the box: connect Tailscale. MWA asks you to sign into Tailscale. Computer: install the Tailscale app and sign in. Railway: paste a Tailscale auth key into the setup page; MWA joins your network as mwa. In your Tailscale admin page, switch on MagicDNS and HTTPS certificates (MWA links you straight to the right page).
  5. MWA checks the box is closed. It shows its private address, https://mwa.<your-tailnet>.ts.net, and runs three checks: the address works, HTTPS works, and no public address is still open. On Railway, it tells you to remove the public domain from the service and re-checks until you have. From then on, that private address is the only door.

Next: put MWA on your phone

Part B: pair your phone (about 3 minutes)

  1. On the phone, install Tailscale from the Play Store or App Store and sign in with the same account. Leave it switched on; it uses very little battery. Once per phone.
  2. On the computer: Settings › Put MWA on your phone. MWA shows a QR code, good for 10 minutes and one phone. Pairing codes can only be made by you: signed in with your password, or on the host computer itself.
  3. Scan it with the phone's camera. It opens your private address with a page that says "Pair this phone with MWA?" and a name to change ("Robert's Pixel"). Tap Pair. The phone now holds its own key in a place web pages can't read.
  4. Tap Install. Android: Chrome offers Install app. iPhone: Share › Add to Home Screen; MWA shows a two-picture guide. The MWA icon is now on your home screen and opens full-screen like any app.
  5. Allow notifications. MWA asks once, explaining what it will send: things that need you, and jobs that finish. Never ads, and it respects your quiet hours.

Part C: connect your things (about 2 minutes, skippable)

  1. "Connect your calendar and email so I can keep an eye on your day." Two big buttons, Google and Outlook, each with one sentence of what it allows ("reads your mail and calendar, writes drafts; never sends"), plus Skip for now.
  2. Sign in on Google's or Microsoft's own page. MWA never sees your password, and the sign-in is kept on MWA's home, not on the phone. Works from the phone or the computer.
  3. Today fills in: your next events, what's worth a look, and a line saying what MWA will do overnight. Skipped? Today shows an example day, and MWA offers to connect when you ask for something that needs it ("Which calendar do you use?").

Everyday use and what-ifs

Every day
Tap the MWA icon. Today, Calendar, Important and Projects open straight away, from the phone's stored copy, then refresh. Notifications bring you to what needs you.
"Can't reach MWA"
The app says why in plain words and still shows the last saved view: Tailscale is off on this phone (one tap to open it), or your computer is asleep or off (things you do will be sent when it's back), or no signal.
I lost my phone
On the computer: Settings › Devices › Remove next to that phone. Its key stops working on its very next request. Also remove the phone in your Tailscale admin page, which takes it off your private network entirely. Its stored copy holds no email bodies or logins.
New phone
Install Tailscale, then Settings › Put MWA on your phone, scan, Pair, Install. About 3 minutes. Remove the old one from the list.
A second person, or a tablet
Pair it the same way; each device has its own key and shows up in the list with when it was last used.
Using it away from home
Works anywhere your phone has signal, as long as Tailscale is on: hotel Wi-Fi, mobile data, abroad. Your connection to MWA is encrypted end to end inside the private network, whatever network you're on.
The computer option and sleep
Set the computer to stay awake when plugged in, or accept that MWA pauses while it sleeps. Jobs due while it was off run when it wakes, and MWA tells you they were late.

Decisions still open

  1. Where should MWA live? My recommendation: Railway, private (Tailscale on, public address off), because you want it reachable anywhere and always running. Your production copy is on Railway today, but public.
  2. Tailscale on your phone: OK to require it? It's the piece that makes MWA invisible to the internet. Without it, MWA has to be a public website protected only by sign-in.
  3. Passkey: add fingerprint or face sign-in for admin, alongside the password?
  4. Tailscale admin settings: switch on MagicDNS and HTTPS certificates. (Your machine's private name appears in a public certificate log; the address still can't be reached by anyone outside your tailnet.)
  5. Google and Microsoft sign-in from a phone: a Google sign-in client set up for the web (published "In production", so sign-ins don't expire every 7 days), and a registered Microsoft app for one-tap Outlook, or keep Outlook as "advanced" for now.
